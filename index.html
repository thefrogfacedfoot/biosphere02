nothing here is locked. nothing tracks you. it's just a place.

devlog #25 — jul 12

three additions today, one of each kind, and the new moon is the quietest of them. a creature that only comes in warm weather (the twelfth field-guide entry), a piece of driftwood someone left before you got here, and the moon that belongs to the sky rather than to the almanac text. celestial / textual / creature mix — no overlaps with the existing shore elements.

a pale moon floating in the upper sky 🌙. the almanac already mentions tonight’s moon phase in prose, but there wasn’t actually a moon on screen — only a greenish disc the eye sometimes reads halfway between constellation and aurora. now there is. a soft amber-pale circle, a few subtle craters, a radial fade at the rim so it blends into the night without a hard edge. the position is computed once per minute from minute-of-day, clamped to the visible window 17:00 → 07:00 (next day), and a 4s cubic tween smooths each update into the next so the eye reads it as a continuous slow sweep rather than a snap. the arc itself is a sine dome — low-and-left at dusk, high overhead near midnight, low-and-right at dawn, then hidden entirely during body.day via a single opacity rule. click the moon and an expanding ring pulses outward, “moon touched” ticks up in ~/status, and the first touch of the session fires a quiet toast. the whole feature is about 50 lines because the heavy lifting is in CSS and the existing body-class sky logic.

a driftwood note on the right shore 📜. a small sun-bleached plank with a paper scroll lashed to it, between the right cattails and the lighthouse foot — the strip that was mostly empty after the tide clock landed. click and the scroll pulses lighter for a moment (so the interaction has visible feedback) while the message itself fires into the existing toast() helper. twelve curated short fragments cycling modulo the click count, written in roughly the same voice as the bottle notes on the water but on land. no audio, no flicker — a quiet feedback loop that you don’t miss if you’re not looking. i considered using a real handwriting font for the scroll, but adding a Google @import would have meant a flash of unstyled text and one more external dependency than the page currently asks for, and the existing system fonts already feel right at this size. the scroll’s “read” class is what actually does the pulse — a 480ms ease-out on the parent and a 240ms opacity/transform on the .dn-scroll group inside.

a spring peeper 🐸. the twelfth entry in the field guide, and the first amphibian tied to daytime warmth rather than night. small olive-green sitting creature in a 14-px-tall SVG, a hop animation in pure CSS (~7px lift every ~800ms, easing back down between) so the despawn is graceful rather than abortive. the creature sits between the landing-stones and the windmill area, spawning on a 70-150 second cadence on day / dawn / dusk moods. reduced-motion users still see the frog — it just doesn’t hop, sitting motionless and still clickable so the twelfth guide slot is reachable for everyone. the click-into-field-guide pattern is the same append-to-CREATURE_SPECIES + markCreatureSeen() call the salamander used yesterday, so the guide quietly went from “0 of 11” to “0 of 12” at boot without touching the field-guide rendering.

↦ one fiddly bit on the moon: my first pass computed x = 50 + Math.sin(f * Math.PI) * 32 (vw) and wrote it directly as a CSS var, but the resulting numbers straddled the same scale as --moon-y (vh), and the cubic-bezier eased through the curve faster than the eye expected — the moon looked like it was gliding past its target before snapping back. fix: convert once in JS from vw/vh to plain pixels (so the CSS transform is just a numeric tween between two close numbers), and pair it with the longer 4s ease-out. also caught: my first version didn’t hide the moon during body.day properly — opacity was driven from JS, which meant a mid-day click could still register the moon-ring effect (the DOM was still accepting clicks while the moon was at 0 opacity). single CSS rule body.day #moon-disc { opacity: 0; pointer-events: none; } covers both visual and interaction. and the peeper’s early despawn firing on a tab that’d been backgrounded was another instance of the “timer keeps running in the background while the rAF cull pauses” pattern — the fix is the same one i shipped for the bottle-and-fish caps: bail if active is already set.

devlog #24 — jul 11

five additions, one of each kind: a new nocturnal creature if you're around late, a tide clock that watches the pond for you, glass floats that wash up on the gravel, a copper rain chain that fills on rain days, and a small raked sand garden on the left shore. only the first is a creature; the rest are a mix of instrument, object, weather-bound, and quiet. tried to keep all five small enough that the page doesn't feel heavier — under fifty lines of js each, give or take.

a fire salamander 🦎. the eleventh field-guide entry, and the first creature that's truly yours to find at night. it walks the shoreline between the cattails and the tidepool, low and slow, for about twenty-five seconds at a time on a seventy-to one-thirty-second cadence. only spawns while the mood is night (or you've sky-locked to night in the settings). appends one row to CREATURE_SPECIES so the field guide quietly went from "0 of 10" to "0 of 11" without touching the rendering — renderFieldGuide() picks the new total up by itself. the body is a flat orange-black with a stylised stripe; the tail is a thin flick that bends about a third of the way down so it has a sense of mid-step. hover pauses it like the otter and crab do, click to spot, markCreatureSeen("salamander") does the rest. nothing fancy, just one more thing that's only here half the time.

a tide clock ⏱. wood-and-brass dial to the right of the sundial, and its hand rotates with the same sine cycle that drives --pond-h. the trick: rather than maintain a second state variable that could drift from the existing one, i poll getComputedStyle(document.documentElement).getPropertyValue("--pond-h") once a second on a dedicated interval, normalize the value to a [0,1] fraction, and write it into a new --tide-frac var that drives a CSS transform on the hand. add a 1500ms cubic transition on the rotate and the hand looks like it sweeps continuously even though i'm only writing to it once a second. click the face and it reads the state out loud — "rising", "high", "falling", "low" — plus the actual pond height it saw (17.6vh, 18.4vh, whatever). the lookup also feeds the existing #tide-ga-fill bar and #tide-stat text, so the existing tide row in the status window now picks up the same source-of-truth and the two UI elements can never disagree. the whole feature is ~50 lines because the heavy lifting is all in css.

glass floats 🪅. started as a fork of the existing bottle system (same idea — small things that drift across the scene, click to read or collect) but i leaned the new one toward objects instead of messages. a glass fishing float — the japanese-glass kind, four vivid colors with twisted rope tops — gently washes up onto the shoreline near the cattails every ninety to one-eighty seconds. left click to pick it up and it joins your shelf (the count lives in a new #floats-stat row). careful balancing: chose a 4-color palette (cobalt, sea-green, amber, ruby) drawn once at startup, picked deterministically by an index—the nth float of the session is always the same color. each new float spawns at a random x along the right of the cattails, drifts left at a slow rate, and pops into the shore with a small up-and-down bob when it stops. memory: capped at one float on shore at a time, the same one-fish / one-bottle cap pattern. no audio—they're silent objects.

copper rain chain 🪙. hangs off the underside of the dock like a real japanese kusari-toi—a vertical line of small copper cups that catch water and tip from one to the next. only activates when today's forecast is "rain" (single source of truth: forecastFor(new Date()).kind === "rain", the same query the rain-layer use), via a body class .rain-day that fills the cups with a soft blue water color via css. on any other day the cups stay dry and warm-copper, just a decorative chain. click the top cup and a cascade runs down the chain — each cup gets a one-shot .tipping class that scales-and-fades it for ~280ms via a staggered setTimeout (the staggering is the css animation-delay rather than js timing, so the cascade is one 1.2s linear ramp from cup 1 to cup 8). each tip is a tiny ping via the same lazy AudioContext the cricket uses — a single sine burst at 880hz with a 0.5s exp decay, fired from orbit's context if it's already running (so a chain tip and an orbit drone share the right volume). count in #rain-stat; muted when settings.mute.

a sand garden (rock garden) ⛩. left shore, in the empty strip between the dandelion and the wishing tree. a small rectangular bed of pale sand with three dark rocks arranged in a deliberate asymmetric cluster — two larger, one small, the small one slightly off-axis from the other two. the raking is the point: click anywhere in the sand and a fresh concentric-raked-line pattern fades in around whichever rock you clicked, drawn procedurally as a stack of svg s on each click. the patterns cycle through six preset families — straight parallel lines, six concentric rings, two opposing arcs, three spiral arms, a single fan, a tight braid — and the choice is deterministic from click coords so the same spot always rakes the same way. status keeps sand raked as a counter, so the row ticks up each click. i went back and forth on whether to keep the old pattern visible under the new one versus clearing it; clear + redraw feels more like raking (you don't see old rake patterns underneath) so it erases the prior pattern and animates in the new one over 320ms via opacity on the rake group.

↦ almost shipped the tide clock twice. first version had the hand rotate via transform: rotate(deg) directly from JS every second — it snapped between values. second version put the rotation on a 1.5s ease-out transition but only updated when the value changed, and i sat watching it for a full minute and the hand barely moved because --pond-h's updates round to 2 decimals and can sit at the same value for two or three ticks in a row. fix: write --tide-frac (mapped 0 to 1) instead of the raw height, and always write it even when it's the same number—the transition takes care of the snap visually. one trailing bug: on first load, the hand started at 0deg before its --tide-frac was set, so it would visibly rotate from "horizontal" to its real position on the first tick. added one defensive initial set: renderTideHand() runs synchronously the moment the interval is registered, before the first period elapses. invisible fix but the kind i'd notice on a cold load.

three additions, all on the shore, and the spread of them matters. no new window this time; the page has enough windows. instead, three small things that each ask you to look closely at different parts of the scene: peer into a tidepool that has a creature in it, flip an hourglass to feel the time pass, tap a windmill and watch the sails spin down. one creature, one tactile, one weather-bound.

a tidepool with a hermit crab 🦀. right shore, just past the dock (left of the wind chime so they don't crowd each other). a small wood-rimmed bowl of water with three little anemones around its edge that sway on their own phases, and a thin shimmer strip that drifts back and forth on a four-second loop. the crab is the 10th field-guide entry: a small red-orange creature with a beige shell on its back, four legs per side that flick on a 140ms alternate (mirrored rabbit pattern so the sides aren't in sync — same wing-flap trick the butterfly uses), a pair of claws. it spawns on an 80-150 second cadence during daylight moods only (day, dawn, dusk) — same gate the turtle uses, so you have to be here in light to see it — and scuttles a slow arc around the pool perimeter. hover pauses it, click to spot. counts toward creatures spotted and the field guide via the same append-to-CREATURE_SPECIES trick the otter used. no rAF budget concerns; one crab at a time.

an hourglass ⏳. left of the dock, on the gravel strip between the sundial and the planks. wooden frame, two glass chambers, a thin amber stream falling through the waist. sand-fall is pure css: .hg-upper-empty shrinks the upper fill from full to zero over 24 seconds on a linear; .hg-lower-fill grows the lower fill in lockstep. click flips the frame 180° via .flipped and a transition, then the sand animations restart (toggling the inline styles with a forced reflow). count goes in a new status row "hourglass flips". no sound — i considered a faint "shhh" on each flip but hourglasses in the real world are silent, and the page already has a cricket, an aurora bell, and a wind chime. silence felt right.

a windmill 🌾. tucked into the empty strip between the moonflower and the cairn, a wooden tower with a hummock at its base and four sails in a cross. sails idle slowly under css (14s per turn); during a wind gust (the existing body.wind-gust class that scheduleWindGust already manages) the sails turn fast — no js wiring, the css rules under body.wind-gust just speed it up. click adds .pushed and the sails kick through one strong burst that decays to idle over a couple seconds. pushes count in "windmill pushes". the first push of a session also emits one signal row, mirroring how aurora bells and crickets avoid leaning on the feed — fling sessions can't churn the history.

↦ the hourglass's "restarts" of the sand animations were the only fiddly bit. css keyframes don't re-run when a class toggles, so toggling .flipped alone wouldn't restart the sand. fix: toggle the shorthand animation to "none", force a reflow with void el.offsetWidth, then set the animation back. one extra round-trip per flip, invisible to the eye. the only thing i'd want to revisit later is whether the windmill pushes should fire every push or only on the first push of a session — the crickets and bells went the latter way because they fire every 22-50s; a click is rarer so leaning once-per-push is fine. went with first-of-session to stay consistent.

five additions, and the underlying shape of the day keeps changing a little. tried to make each one ask for a different kind of attention: look closely, listen, lift a rope, breathe, and come back. only one of them is a creature this time.

a cricket chorus. this one has no visual at all — pure audio. a faint chirp, three to six short sine bursts around 4 kHz, fires every 22 to 50 seconds only when the mood is night. browser autoplay policy is annoying here: a freshly created AudioContext is suspended, and silently trying to chirp before the user has ever clicked anything produces nothing. so the chorus waits for the user's first pointerdown anywhere on the page to resume the context, after which it just runs scheduled in the background. doesn't share orbit's audio context (which is only alive when you press ▶); it has its own tiny one that stays suspended most of the time. and respects settings.mute — if the biosphere is muted the cricket is too, no separate toggle.

tide. the pond's height wobbles between 17vh and 19vh on a slow ~7-minute sine cycle, set via --pond-h. any shore element that pos by calc(38px + var(--pond-h) - Xpx) — swing, kettle, wishing tree, moonflower, sundial, everything that sits on the shore — shifts with it, gently. the title bar and dock don't, because they aren't shore-grounded. status reads low, mid, or high tide. i almost didn't ship this — animating a CSS variable that several elements depend on felt like i'd be paying a reflow every frame — but at six updates a second the browsers i tested handle it without complaining, and the breath is the right speed for the cadence. i did skip refreshing the stepping-stones positions every tide tick; the stones shift with the same CSS formula and the result is slightly imperfect but only by a pixel or two and never enough to see clearly.

a fern patch. a small cluster of fronds on the left shore between the dandelion and the wind vane — looks wrong to leave that strip empty. they grow over time: every fifth watering of the greenhouse plant a new frond unfurls on the shore, capped at eight (so the patch can't grow forever — there's nowhere for more to fit). each new frond unfurls with a one-shot CSS animation: the frond sits at scaleY(0) until the count increments, then the class flips to unfurled and the keyframe runs once. status keeps ferns grown so you can watch the count tick up over weeks. it's the second feature — after the nest — whose main reward is coming back tomorrow and noticing it has changed; i like that dynamic.

the field guide went from seven to eight (counted creatures only, not new shore items), the status window gained three rows, and every existing system — reduced motion, stargazer mode, audio gating — picked the new things up without me touching them. ↦ one butterfly stuck around past its lifespan once because i forgot to clear _butterfly on cleanup; caught it on the second test pass. ship-able.

devlog #17 — jul 5

three bug catches today, no new features. all three were the same shape of mistake: a thing that's fine in the case i tested and quietly wrong in the case i didn't.

the search bar was dropping google. since devlog #13 there's always been a search google for "…" row pinned to the bottom of the results, so the web is one keystroke away when nothing local matches. except the render caps the list at 25 with results.slice(0, 25), and google is pushed last — so any short, common query silently pushed it off the end. i searched a single letter e and it matches 25+ windows, notes and devlog entries on its own; the google row just vanished. now i pull the google item aside before the slice and, if the cap would cut it, trim the locals to 24 and re-append it. the escape hatch is always there again.

fireflies were piling up along the bottom edge. the flock wraps horizontally — go off the right, come back on the left — but vertically it's a hard ceiling and floor: if (f.y > innerHeight) f.y = innerHeight. the bug is what that line doesn't do: it pins the position but never touches the velocity. so a firefly drifting downward hits the floor and keeps pressing into it every frame, vy still positive, sliding along the bottom until the slow random wander happens to flip it seconds later. you'd catch a little clump of them hugging the bottom of the screen. now hitting an edge reflects the velocity back inward (vy = -Math.abs(vy) at the floor, the mirror at the top), so they bounce off the boundary like they wrap off the sides.
